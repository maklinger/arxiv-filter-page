Suppression of the Laser Hosing Instability in a Parabolic Plasma Channel

Yash M Jogiya, Vishwa Bandhu Pathak

We present an analytical stability analysis of the laser hosing instability in a parabolic channel in the weakly relativistic regime. We employ a variational principle approach to derive a set of coupled equations describing laser dynamics in a transversely inhomogeneous plasma. The quadratic Lagrangian formalism is applied to perform a stability analysis and to derive the growth rates for hosing in both long- and short-wavelength regimes. We find that a parabolic channel suppresses the long-wavelength hosing mode completely, for any channel depth, and that the unstable band of the short-wavelength mode is bounded below by a cutoff wavenumber set by the ratio of the channel frequency to the hosing coupling. A steeper channel narrows the unstable band and lowers the short-wavelength growth rate, but cannot stabilize the mode, since the dispersion relation forbids damping identically. The analysis is accompanied by two-dimensional particle-in-cell simulations, which reproduce the prediction in the nonlinear regime: the long-wavelength mode is absent in a channel, the surviving modulation shortens as the channel steepens, and its amplitude falls with channel depth. Further, our simulation results demonstrate the sensitivity of off-axis laser propagation to the plasma channel and distinguish the hosing instability from laser oscillations due to off-axis laser propagation in a plasma channel.

Can Magnetic Reconnection Accelerate Energetic Particles in the Heliosheath?

Jeongbhin Seo, Teresa Boland, Fan Guo, Xiaocan Li, Giulia Murtas

The mechanism for accelerating energetic particles in the outer heliosphere is still under debate. The termination shock, turbulence, and magnetic reconnection have all been proposed as viable mechanisms. We investigate compression-driven particle acceleration during magnetic reconnection in the heliospheric current sheet using the MHD--SDE framework that incorporates energetic-particle feedback into the MHD evolution. For the heliosheath conditions considered here, we find that reconnection-driven acceleration is inefficient downstream of the termination shock, whereas reconnection near the heliopause can generate stronger compression and produce a power-law differential intensity spectrum $J(\varepsilon)\propto\varepsilon^{-5.4}$. However, even the hardest spectrum achieved during magnetic reconnection remains much steeper than the typical $J(\varepsilon)\propto\varepsilon^{-1.5}$ spectrum observed in the heliosheath. This indicates that magnetic reconnection alone is insufficient to explain the acceleration of energetic particles in the heliosheath.

Influence of Laguerre-Gaussian pulse mode purity on positron acceleration in nonlinear wakefields

Lars Reichwein

The acceleration of positrons in wakefields remains a crucial challenge. One proposal has been to use Laguerre-Gaussian laser pulses as drivers, which provide corresponding accelerating and focusing regions due to their inherent field structure. Here, we study the effect of mode order and mode purity on the acceleration process by means of particle-in-cell simulations. Longitudinal-field particle acceleration in channels generated by strong electromagnetic waves in pair plasma

Kavin Tangtartharakul, Andrei M. Beloborodov, Carolina Herrera Hernandez, Mihail O. Cernaianu, Alexey Arefiev

Relativistically strong electromagnetic waves interacting with pair plasmas appear in astrophysical settings and may become accessible in future laboratory experiments using high-power lasers. Transverse modulations and filamentation can turn extended wavefronts into structures of a finite transverse width. We examine the interaction of such a finite-width wave incident on a cold, uniform pair plasma using a three-dimensional kinetic particle-in-cell simulation. The wave does not penetrate the plasma and instead advances by creating a fully evacuated channel. The wave loses energy at a rate that cannot be explained by plasma clearing alone. We find that the excess losses are caused by strong particle acceleration near the walls of the channel. The acceleration is driven by a longitudinal electric field associated with the transverse wave structure and the geometry of the self-generated channel.

Electromagnetic signatures of gradual magnetic dissipation in jets from supermassive black holes

Stavros Dimitrakoudis, Maria Petropoulou, Stamatios I. Stathopoulos, Dimitrios Giannios

Context. Blazar jets -- collimated and relativistic plasma outflows viewed at small angles -- are prolific sources of non-thermal radiation. Aims. We develop a numerical framework for calculating the emission from an extended blazar jet where gradual energy dissipation, mediated through magnetic reconnection, takes place across a wide range of distances from the supermassive black hole. Methods. We adapt the open-source radiative transfer code LeHaMoC that self-consistently calculates photon spectra from relativistic charged particles that occupy a spherical blob to treat the emission from an extended jet. The jet is treated as a series of segments that interact with each other through radiative transfer. The effects of radiation spillage are calculated iteratively throughout the jet, accounting for relativistic effects arising from the relative motion between jet segments, until a steady state is reached. Results. We find that this approach produces some distinct differences in the total spectral energy distribution compared to the results obtained by adding the emission escaping from each constituent segment of the jet (optical thin regime), increasing the part of the spectrum affected by inverse Compton scattering by a factor of three. However, the latter is always far less luminous than the synchrotron component, even when enhanced by external radiation fields. Conclusions. Gradual magnetic dissipation in jets from supermassive black holes leads to nearly universal, synchrotron-dominated photon spectra. The numerical framework developed here is readily adaptable to a broad range of jet dissipation scenarios and applications to extended jet emission.

Radiative relativistic reconnection powering the hard-state emission of X-ray binaries: synchrotron-driven pair loading, MeV tail, and seed photons

Valeriia Rohoza, Maria Petropoulou, Hayk Hakobyan, Despina Karavola, Lorenzo Sironi

In the hard state, X-ray emission from black hole X-ray binaries is attributed to Comptonization in the corona, a highly magnetized region near the black hole. Relativistic magnetic reconnection drives bulk motions that can upscatter soft photons and reproduce the spectral peak at $\sim 100$ keV for parameters relevant to Cygnus X-1. However, the origin of the seed photons and of the MeV tail above the peak remains unclear. In 3D reconnection, so-called "free" particles escape the flux ropes and accelerate at the maximum rate set by the reconnection electric field. Their synchrotron emission extends to tens of MeV, which enables $γγ$ pair production with the Compton-upscattered photons. We present 3D particle-in-cell simulations of relativistic reconnection in pair plasma, in which particles cool by inverse Compton (IC) scattering below the synchrotron self-absorption threshold and by synchrotron radiation above it. Of the incoming magnetic power, $\sim 40 \%$ is converted to IC radiation and $\gtrsim 10$-$20\%$ to synchrotron. Using analytical estimates and radiative calculations for Cygnus X-1, we model how the radiation from reconnection-accelerated particles is reprocessed in the corona. Pair production attenuates the synchrotron emission above $\sim 1$ MeV, and the escaping $γ$-rays form an MeV tail consistent with the observations, extending up to at least $\sim 10$ MeV. Most of the absorbed power goes into secondary pairs, whose Thomson optical depth can reach order unity. Their synchrotron emission, peaking at $\sim 0.1 \, \text{keV}$, can supply the seed photons for Comptonization. Our results support reconnection-based synchrotron self-Compton models of the hard-state emission in X-ray binaries.

The preplasma origin of the plasma-mirror reflectivity response

Zhongwei Wang, Douglass Schumacher

Plasma mirrors (PM) enhance the temporal contrast of high-power lasers, but declining reflectivity at subrelativistic intensities limits their energy throughput. Here we show that prepulse-generated preplasma drives this decline in p polarization primarily through absorption. Particle-in-cell simulations using a measured prepulse profile reproduce the reflectivity decrease, with near- and far-field energy accounting showing a weak contribution from scattering. Optical-cycle-resolved electron tracking reveals successive electron bunches extracted from the plasma surface, supporting a vacuum-heating interpretation. Under the simulated conditions, s-polarized absorption remains weak and no comparable decline occurs. Applying the model to two representative double-plasma-mirror (DPM) systems, BELLA and CoReLS, it captures both the p-polarized and s-polarized reflectivity responses across the operating range. Yet, the model still misses the high-fluence s-polarized reflectivity rollover, and further investigation of the scattering channel might be needed. These findings connect prepulse history to polarization-dependent energy loss, informing the choice of plasma-mirror operating conditions.

High-Efficiency Wakefield Acceleration of Positrons via a Slit Plasma Channel

Yu-Jie Wang, Xing-Long Zhu, Hong-Kang Zuo, Zheng-Ming Sheng

Although plasma-based wakefield acceleration has achieved remarkable success for electrons, efficient positron acceleration remains a long standing challenge, as positrons suffer severe defocusing in the accelerating phases of such structures. Here, we propose a slit plasma wakefield acceleration (SPWA) scheme, in which a relativistic electron beam propagating through a slit channel formed by two parallel plasma slabs excites crossing plasma-electron filamentary structures within the slit, generating intense wakefields tailored to simultaneously provide longitudinal acceleration and transverse focusing for a trailing positron bunch. A theoretical model is developed to characterize the wakefield structure responsible for positron acceleration and focusing. Three-dimensional particle-in-cell simulations validate the model, and demonstrate that the SPWA scheme achieves a positron trapping efficiency of nearly 100%, while maintaining low emittance growth and narrow energy spread for an injected positron beam. These findings establish a new and robust approach to high-efficiency positron acceleration, with promising implications for future compact electron-positron colliders and high-energy positron applications.

On 21 November 2024 at 17:55 UTC, a solar flare erupted from far-side active region (AR) 13892, with enhanced hard X-ray flux measured by Solar Orbiter (SO). It was associated with a coronal mass ejection (CME) travelling at 1436 km/s along the limb. Structure of energy flux densities in Earth's magnetotail reconnection regions: Bridging simulation and observation

Cara L. Waters, Jonathan P. Eastwood, Harry C. Lewis, Naïs Fargette, Marit Øieroset, Tai D. Phan, David L. Newman, Martin V. Goldman

Magnetic reconnection is important in both space and laboratory contexts because it changes the topology of magnetic fields and converts magnetic energy into particle energies. Establishing where this energy conversion occurs is critical for understanding how reconnection partitions energy. Using a novel machine learning approach based on simulations that explicitly identifies the separatrix regions for the first time, the partition of energy flux densities within the magnetic reconnection site is evaluated through statistical study of magnetotail reconnection events observed in situ. A magnetically heated slim disc-corona model for tidal disruption events

Wei Chen, Chris Done, Erlin Qiao, Zikun Lin, Chichuan Jin

Tidal disruption events (TDEs) can reach super-Eddington luminosities near their peak. While their optical/UV emission may arise from multiple processes, X-rays are more likely to be produced by the innermost accretion flow close to the black hole, analogous to super-Eddington flows in stellar-mass X-ray binaries (XRBs) and active galactic nuclei (AGNs). Super-Eddington AGNs typically show disc-dominated spectra, with weak and steep coronae, while super-Eddington XRBs show a wider variety of spectral shapes. Here we explore disc-corona coupling in TDEs by developing a super-Eddington ($\dot{m}>1$: slim) disc model in which magnetic reconnection transfers some of the accretion power from the disc into a hot Comptonizing corona. We test the model on the well-monitored X-ray TDE AT2021ehb, and can broadly fit the brightest X-ray spectra with a black-hole mass $M_{\rm BH}\sim10^6$--$10^7\,M_\odot$ at $\dot m\sim 1$, but need variable magnetic coupling to the disc to match the large observed fast changes in coronal contribution. AGN at similarly high luminosity do not show such variable coronae, but the transient XRB outbursts do, suggesting that it may be a common feature of rapidly changing, non-steady accretion flows. As the TDE declines to $\dot{m}\sim 0.01$, the source hardens, but never goes below $Γ\sim 2$, unlike both AGN and XRB at these luminosities. Thus the X-ray spectral evolution in this TDE does not resemble either XRB or AGN in detail, likely pointing to the very different, highly variable nature of the accretion flow in a tidal disruption event.

We analyze 103 hours of high-cadence (4 samples/s) in-situ Solar Orbiter ion data, collected from July 2020 to September 2022, which correspond mainly to slow and Alfvénic solar wind. The Birth of Plasmoids: Weibel-Driven Secondary Tearing and its Suppression in Relativistic Reconnection

Matthew Goodbred, Anatoly Spitkovsky

Plasmoids, or magnetic islands, dominate relativistic reconnection layers in kinetic simulations, yet the instability that creates them remains poorly understood. By tracking newborn islands in 2D particle-in-cell simulations of pair-plasma reconnection with magnetizations $0.1\leσ_0\le1000$, we show that plasmoids are seeded by a current-filamentation/Weibel instability driven by counter-streaming out-of-plane current carriers near primary x-lines. Measured growth rates are $\sim0.1$ times the background plasma frequency and agree with linear theory evaluated on the simulated distributions. As inflowing plasma loads the sheet away from the x-line, the current is carried by more particles and their out-of-plane drift slows. The filamentation instability shuts off once this drift becomes comparable to the momentum spread in the outflow direction, which occurs at a distance $\ell_{\rm iso}\proptoσ_0$ from the x-line. Because the unstable region extends farther at higher $σ_0$, our results account for the onset of plasmoid-dominated reconnection in the relativistic regime but predict that plasmoid formation is suppressed beyond $\sim\ell_{\rm iso}$ from primary x-lines, even in ultra-relativistic reconnection. Particle acceleration, variability, and coherent radio emission attributed to plasmoid-dominated reconnection may therefore be confined to smaller regions than previously assumed.

First-principles simulation of free-electron lasers with the Galilean electromagnetic particle-in-cell method: even harmonics of planar undulators

Alexander Pukhov

Free-electron laser (FEL) codes average the electron motion over the undulator period and add the radiation harmonics through on-axis coupling factors. This is accurate for the fundamental, but the even harmonics of a planar undulator, which vanish on the axis, are not modelled at all. We show that the Galilean electromagnetic particle-in-cell method (GEM-PIC), which resolves the electron motion within the undulator period and the carrier of the forward radiation field without a Courant limit, simulates FELs on two CPU cores at two to three times the cost of a time-dependent run of the averaged code GENESIS. The quasi-cylindrical code GEM-PIC-RZ reproduces the coherent spontaneous emission of a planar undulator in each azimuthal mode $m = 0$--3 to within 2\,\% of the radiation integral, and seeded amplifiers to within a few per cent of GENESIS run with the same physics. For a seeded planar FEL at 10.6\,nm it predicts 12\,MW of second-harmonic power at saturation, $1.1\times10^{-3}$ of the fundamental; this harmonic is absent from the averaged model. The unaveraged code Puffin, which has no space charge, gives a comparable level, 9 to 11\,MW for the space-charge-widened and the initial beam size, and the same transverse structure.

Stabilization of kinetic plasma instabilities via instantaneous external field control

Jiyoung Yoo, Jingwei Hu

We propose an instantaneous control framework for the stabilization of kinetic plasma instabilities using external fields. The control problem is formulated as a PDE-constrained optimization that accounts for nonlinear plasma dynamics, while the control is determined at each time step to avoid global-in-time optimization. The Discretize-then-Optimize (DtO) approach is adopted, with the forward equations first discretized using a particle-in-cell (PIC) method. The subsequent control computation is derived from the discrete particle update, ensuring consistency with the forward solver. The control objective is formulated in terms of electromagnetic field energy and low-order moments rather than the full distribution function, making the optimization more amenable to practical implementation. The framework is applied to both electrostatic and electromagnetic settings and evaluated on several benchmark problems, including the two-stream, bump-on-tail, Dory--Guest--Harris, and Weibel instabilities. Across these tests, the control successfully suppresses field energy growth and keeps the distribution function close to its initial state. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed strategy in suppressing kinetic plasma instabilities.

These eruptions include shock waves driven by coronal mass ejections (CMEs) and magnetic reconnection in solar flares. Disentangling these source mechanisms within the measurements has been a long-standing problem for space physics researchers. Stabilization of counter propagating currents by energy conservation in collisions

Thomas Kluge, Jannis Schulz, Arthur Hirsch-Passicos, Pawel Ordyna, Ulrich Schramm

Current filamentation generates magnetic fields and shapes electron transport in dense plasmas, where collisions can either suppress or enhance its growth. Here we show that conserving electron kinetic energy in a collisional model can change both the dominant instability and its characteristic wavelength. We develop a linear kinetic theory for drifting, anisotropic electron populations with a relaxation operator that conserves particle number and laboratory-frame kinetic energy while allowing momentum transfer to a stationary ion background. For counterstreaming currents, energy conservation maintains the stabilizing influence of charge separation over a broader range of collision frequencies than a model conserving particle number alone. NEOPIC: A Neural Operator Framework for Particle-based Kinetic Plasma Simulations

Sriramkrishnan Muralikrishnan, Chelsea Maria John, Jeffrey Kelling, Michael Bussmann, Robert Speck, Paul Gibbon

We introduce NEOPIC, a framework for learning the field operator in particle-based methods applied to kinetic plasma simulations. Its key novelty is a neural operator that acts directly on the empirical measure of the particles, rather than on a grid, and is evaluated at the particle positions. No mesh or tree is needed and, unlike grid-based neural operators, no deposition onto a mesh takes place, so the particle noise that dominates the inputs of particle simulations is not aliased. Since it replaces only the field solve, it serves as a plug-and-play field solver for particle-based methods: it can be trained on data from particle-in-cell, particle-in-Fourier or tree codes, and combined with any time integrator, including implicit schemes.
